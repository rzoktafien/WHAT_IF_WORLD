## 1. Research question and scenario
**What If:** Bagaimana jika permukaan lahan gambut tampak tidak lagi terbakar, tetapi pembakaran membara masih bertahan di lapisan gambut bawah permukaan?

**FICTIONAL_ASSUMPTION:** A localized ignition starts a subsurface smouldering front in degraded tropical peatland. This scenario uses a documented physical mechanism; it is not a claim about a named current incident or any specific hotspot.
Changed variable: presence of a self-sustaining subsurface smouldering front.
Held constant: local peat properties, moisture distribution, topography, weather and ignition geometry are bounded scenario conditions, not universal Indonesian conditions.
Geographic scope: Indonesian tropical peatland as a broad setting; direct field evidence cited here is from one degraded peatland site in Sumatra.
Time horizon: hours to days are plausible within the experiment; its 4–10 day observation interval must not be universalized.
Human stakes: smoke/air-quality exposure, detection and suppression challenges, and damage to peat biological systems. No quantified health-outcome claim is made.

## 2. Evidence separation
### Current context — not proof of an active peat fire
BMKG's 5 November 2026 outlook reported high-confidence satellite hotspots in the preceding ten days in Papua Selatan (685), Sumatera Selatan (168), and Sulawesi Tenggara (167), plus smoke observed in parts of Jambi, Kalimantan Tengah, Maluku, and Papua Selatan. BMKG explicitly cautions that hotspots do not always indicate an actual fire. This does not establish that these detections are peat fires or subsurface smouldering events.

### Scientific mechanism
The peer-reviewed GAMBUT field experiment in degraded peatland in Sumatra documented flameless smouldering, persistence over 4–10 days in experimental plots, continued deeper smouldering despite three rainfall events, average burn depth around 25 cm, and severe thermal impact to peat biological systems at depths up to 30 cm. Results are site- and experiment-specific.

### Editorial judgment
The strongest story is the contrast between a subdued surface and subsurface smouldering. Do not imply every peat fire is invisible, rainfall never helps, a hotspot proves a fire, or all Indonesian peatlands behave like the experimental site.

## 5. Scientific model and red-team review
- Baseline: Peat is accumulated organic soil; under suitable conditions it can support smouldering combustion.
- Mechanism: Combustion can continue within porous organic material below the surface without a sustained visible flame front. GAMBUT measured a drying front preceding smouldering spread and different responses between shallow and deeper layers.
- Immediate effect: A localized subsurface hot spot forms; surface visual cues may under-represent activity at depth.
- Delayed effect: The smouldering front may spread slowly through suitable peat conditions and produce smoke/emissions; exact spread and duration depend on local peat properties, moisture, ignition and weather.
- Rainfall: Can reduce or suppress shallow activity; GAMBUT observed deeper smouldering continuing after its rainfall events. Do not depict rain as useless.
- Scope: experimental evidence is from degraded peatland in Sumatra, not all Indonesian peatlands.
- Unknowns: no site-specific live underground-fire detection data, forecast, quantified health-risk estimate, or national extent estimate is used.
- Red-team: hotspot ≠ confirmed fire; confirmed karhutla ≠ confirmed peat fire; peat fire ≠ always invisible; 4–10 days ≠ universal duration; 25 cm/30 cm are experimental findings; rain can affect shallow layers; synthetic visuals require hypothetical framing.
