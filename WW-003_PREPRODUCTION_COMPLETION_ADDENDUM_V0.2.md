## 3. Scientific model — bounded scenario

- Baseline: thirst behavior, AVP-mediated renal water conservation, and ongoing water losses are coordinated but distinguishable processes.
- Changed variable: conscious perception of thirst is selectively suppressed in a fictional thought experiment.
- Held constant only as a scenario rule: no specific lesion, diagnosis, or treatment is assigned. AVP secretion and renal response are not silently declared normal; they remain separate variables.
- Immediate effect: the character does not experience the usual conscious urge to seek water. No physiological crisis is shown as an immediate consequence.
- Delayed effects: not quantified. Consequences depend on baseline hydration, ongoing losses, environment, exertion, access to water, and individual physiology.
- Spatial scope: one anonymous adult in a non-identifiable domestic interior; no population-level inference.
- Story time horizon: 57 seconds of editorial runtime. This is not a 57-second or 24-hour physiological prediction.
- Feedback/secondary effects: omitted unless independently supported; no deterministic heat illness, hypernatremia, collapse, or organ injury sequence.
- Known unknowns: no reviewed controlled human study establishes a healthy person's isolated thirst suppression for exactly 24 hours.
- Alternative explanations: clinical adipsia can coexist with AVP deficiency; case reports demonstrate heterogeneity but cannot predict the hypothetical character's physiology.
- Numbers/units: no dehydration percentage, serum sodium value, or time-to-collapse value is approved.
- Removed claim: any assertion that loss of thirst inevitably causes severe dehydration or death after a fixed interval remains REJECTED.
