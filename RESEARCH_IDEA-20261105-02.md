## 1. Research question and editorial framing

**Selected What If question:** “Bisakah batu Venus menyimpan jejak air purba?”

**Scientific core question:** Could certain hydrous minerals formed during a potentially wetter period survive or leave recognizable chemical signatures after exposure to Venus’ present hot, high-pressure atmosphere?

**Recommended framing:** A laboratory experiment published on 2026-10-30 showed that selected minerals change under simulated Venus surface conditions. Fluorine-bearing micas may be more thermally stable than their hydroxyl-bearing counterparts. If comparable minerals are eventually detected on Venus and their geological origin is constrained, they could help scientists test hypotheses about the planet’s past water and volatile cycles.

**Important distinction:**
- FACT: specific reactions occurred in selected samples in a laboratory simulation.
- EXTRAPOLATION: those reactions may help predict which minerals could persist on Venus.
- CONDITIONAL INFERENCE: future detection of suitable minerals, with geological context, could provide clues to past water.
- NOT ESTABLISHED: those minerals have been detected on Venus; Venus definitely had oceans; Venus was habitable; or life existed there.

**Do not frame as:**
- “Scientists found ancient ocean rocks on Venus.”
- “Venus definitely had oceans for billions of years.”
- “Magnesite proves Venus once had liquid water.”
- “The experiment recreated Venus’s entire geological history.”
- “Fluorine preserves all water-bearing minerals indefinitely.”
- “Venus once had life.”

## 2. Scientific model and mechanism

### 2.1 Present-day surface boundary conditions

Venus’ surface is approximately 460–467 °C and around 92–93 bar near the reference planetary radius. The atmosphere is dominated by carbon dioxide and contains reactive minor gases, including sulfur-bearing species and trace water/hydrogen fluoride. Conditions vary with elevation.

These values explain why surface-atmosphere reactions and mineral stability are central to Venus geology. They do not imply every location has identical conditions.

### 2.2 The 2026 GEER experiment

Port et al. exposed six selected minerals—calcite, siderite, magnesite, phlogopite, muscovite, and brucite—to simulated Venus conditions in NASA Glenn Research Center’s Glenn Extreme Environment Rig (GEER).

Reported conditions:
- Duration: 60 consecutive days.
- Temperature: 460 ± 0 °C.
- Pressure: 93 ± 1 bar.
- Atmosphere: simulated mixture of major and minor Venus gases.
- Characterization: X-ray diffraction (XRD), X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), microscopy/spectroscopy and thermodynamic calculations.

The experiment is a controlled laboratory test. It is not a direct sample from Venus and does not simulate every geological timescale or process.

### 2.3 Two mineral reactions relevant to the story

**A. Brucite → magnesite/periclase**
- Brucite (Mg(OH)2) underwent substantial alteration.
- XRD identified magnesite (MgCO3) and periclase (MgO) after exposure.
- Residual brucite remained; the reaction had not completed after 60 days.
- The authors describe dehydration followed by reaction with CO2 in the simulated atmosphere.
- Critical caveat: magnesite formed in this dry laboratory environment from a hydrous starting mineral. Its formation in this experiment is not proof of past surface liquid water on Venus. Magnesite has possible formation pathways that require separate geological evaluation.

**B. Hydrous micas → fluorine-bearing micas**
- Phlogopite and muscovite reacted with hydrogen fluoride (HF).
- The study reports partial substitution of structural hydroxyl (OH) by fluorine (F), producing fluorophlogopite and fluoromuscovite.
- Fluorine-bearing end members can be more thermally stable than hydroxyl-bearing forms.
- If hydrous micas formed during an earlier water-richer period, fluorination could help preserve mineralogical traces.
- Critical caveat: the proposed preservation pathway is conditional. It is not proof that such minerals exist on Venus today or that their original formation necessarily records an ocean.

### 2.4 How mineral clues could inform water history

A defensible inference chain is:

1. Some hydrous minerals form in geological processes involving fluids or water.
2. Venus’ present surface environment can alter or dehydrate exposed minerals.
3. The 2026 experiment indicates that fluorine substitution can occur in selected micas under simulated Venus conditions.
4. Fluorine substitution may improve the thermal stability of some hydrous mica phases.
5. If a suitable mineral assemblage is detected on Venus, its composition, texture, geological setting and competing formation pathways could help constrain past water/volatile conditions.

Every step after the laboratory observation is conditional. Mineral presence alone would not establish a global ocean, habitability, or life.

### 2.5 Broader context and disagreement

- Pioneer Venus measurements found a strongly elevated atmospheric deuterium-to-hydrogen (D/H) ratio. Donahue et al. interpreted this as consistent with substantial past water loss.
- Grinspoon later proposed an alternative steady-state interpretation involving continuing hydrogen sources and escape. Therefore, D/H is an indirect constraint whose interpretation depends on source and escape assumptions.
- Way et al. (2016) used a 3-D climate model to show that selected early-Venus assumptions could permit moderate temperatures. The result depends on assumed rotation, topography, atmospheric composition and water inventory.
- These strands are context, not direct confirmation of the 2026 mineral experiment’s historical interpretation.
