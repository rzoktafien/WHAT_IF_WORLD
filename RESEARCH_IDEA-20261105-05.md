## 1. Research question and editorial framing

**Selected What If question:** “Bagaimana jika dunia tidak pernah sepakat membatasi bahan kimia perusak ozon?”

**Editorial angle:** The Montreal Protocol is a rare, measurable case in which international cooperation changed the trajectory of a global environmental risk. The current 2026 Antarctic ozone hole supplies a timely visual anchor; peer-reviewed modelling supplies a bounded alternate-world scenario.

**Core contrast:**
1. ACTUAL WORLD: ozone-depleting substances (ODS) were progressively controlled under the Montreal Protocol, adopted in 1987 and in force from 1989.
2. MEASURED 2026: the Antarctic ozone hole was still large and ranked seventh by seasonal mean area in 48 years of monitoring, while the long-term recovery trajectory remained on track.
3. MODELLED WORLD AVOIDED: a 2009 coupled chemistry-climate model simulated continued ODS production growth of 3% annually in a world where ODS were never regulated. It projected 17% global column-ozone depletion by 2020 and 67% by 2065 relative to 1980, with severe polar depletion becoming year-round.
4. INTERPRETATION: the no-regulation model is an extreme counterfactual, not a forecast of what would certainly have happened. It depends on its emissions scenario and coupled model assumptions.

**Recommended scientific framing:** “The world avoided a much more severe ozone crisis by regulating ODS.” Keep the counterfactual labelled as a model simulation and attribute its assumptions and dates.

## 2. Scientific model

### 2.1 What the ozone hole is
The Antarctic ozone hole is a seasonal region of markedly depleted stratospheric ozone, conventionally mapped as the area with total-column ozone below 220 Dobson Units (DU) south of 40°S in NASA's ozone-hole-area data product. It is not a physical opening in the atmosphere. Stratospheric ozone absorbs harmful ultraviolet radiation and helps protect life at Earth's surface.

### 2.2 Chemical mechanism
1. Long-lived human-made ODS, including CFCs and halons, are emitted near the surface and transported into the stratosphere.
2. Ultraviolet radiation breaks down these compounds and releases chlorine- and bromine-containing reactive species.
3. During the Antarctic winter, a strong polar vortex isolates very cold stratospheric air. Polar stratospheric clouds (PSCs) form under these cold conditions.
4. Reactions on PSC particle surfaces convert relatively inactive chlorine reservoir compounds into photochemically active forms.
5. When sunlight returns in Antarctic spring, photolysis releases reactive chlorine; chlorine and bromine participate in catalytic cycles that destroy ozone while the halogen species are regenerated.
6. Warming and weakening of the polar vortex, together with mixing with surrounding air, eventually reduces the special conditions that sustain rapid seasonal depletion.

**Important nuance:** ODS abundance is the long-term chemical driver, but year-to-year ozone-hole area and depth also depend strongly on atmospheric dynamics, especially polar-vortex strength, temperature, and planetary-wave activity. The large 2026 area was associated with an unusually powerful/large polar vortex and weak wave activity, not a sudden reversal of the Montreal Protocol.

### 2.3 What the 2026 measurements actually say
- Seasonal mean area during 7 September–13 October 2026: about 9.6 million square miles (25 million km²).
- Rank: seventh-largest seasonal mean area in 48 years of NOAA/NASA monitoring.
- One-day maximum extent: 18 September 2026, about 10.5 million square miles (27.1 million km²).
- NOAA's South Pole balloon-borne measurement found the lowest directly overhead ozone concentration of the year was 105 DU on 17 October 2026. This point measurement is not the same metric as the satellite-derived area.
- NOAA/NASA explicitly state that the long-term recovery trajectory remains on track, despite the unusually large 2026 seasonal area.

Do not mix the seasonal mean, one-day maximum area, and local column-ozone concentration as though they were interchangeable measurements.

### 2.4 What the 2026 counterfactual estimate means
NASA ozone scientist Paul Newman told NOAA that the 2026 ozone hole could have been approximately 1–2 million square miles larger if ODS abundances were still as high as their year-2000 peak. This is an attributed estimate, not an independent measurement or a separately documented full scenario model in the release. Keep the attribution and conditional wording every time it is used.

### 2.5 The deeper no-regulation scenario
Newman et al. (2009), in a peer-reviewed Atmospheric Chemistry and Physics paper, used a fully coupled radiation-chemical-dynamical model to simulate a world in which ODS were never regulated and production increased by 3% per year. In that particular “world avoided” scenario, the model projected:
- 17% loss of globally averaged column ozone by 2020 relative to 1980;
- 67% loss by 2065 relative to 1980;
- severe polar ozone depletion becoming year-round rather than seasonal;
- tropical lower-stratospheric ozone remaining relatively stable until about 2053 and then collapsing toward near zero by 2058 in that simulation;
- erythemal ultraviolet radiation more than doubling in northern summer mid-latitudes by 2060.

These are model outputs under a specified emissions pathway, not observations and not a deterministic forecast. The scenario assumes sustained annual ODS production growth and should be presented as “one published modelled world avoided,” not as the single inevitable outcome of no treaty.

### 2.6 Recovery timeline and uncertainty
The WMO 2022 Scientific Assessment states that, under continued compliance with the Montreal Protocol, Antarctic total-column ozone is expected to return to 1980 values around 2066. The reported model range is approximately 2049–2077, reflecting modelled dynamical variability. This is a return-to-1980 benchmark for total-column ozone, not a promise that every Antarctic ozone-hole metric disappears in that year. The same assessment describes continued interannual variability and remaining scientific challenges.

### 2.7 Treaty facts
The Montreal Protocol was adopted in Montreal on 16 September 1987 and entered into force on 1 January 1989. The UN Treaty Collection listed 198 parties as of its 3 November 2026 status page. UNEP describes the agreement as a phase-out framework for production and consumption of ODS. Avoid claiming that the treaty eliminated every ozone-related risk or that every replacement chemical is harmless; the Kigali Amendment separately phases down HFCs primarily for climate reasons.

## 5. Scientific red-team and exclusions

1. **Area is not depth.** A large ozone-hole area does not necessarily mean the lowest ozone concentration or the deepest depletion. Keep the 25 million km² seasonal mean, 27.1 million km² one-day maximum, and 105 DU South Pole point measurement distinct.
2. **Weather is not treaty reversal.** The 2026 large area is attributed in part to a strong polar vortex and weak wave activity. Do not suggest ODS concentrations suddenly rose in 2026.
3. **Counterfactuals are not observations.** The 1–2 million square mile estimate is attributed to Paul Newman. The 2009 “world avoided” scenario uses a separate 3% annual ODS growth assumption. Never merge those two estimates into one calculation.
4. **No false certainty.** The 2009 model is one peer-reviewed coupled-model scenario. Use “dalam simulasi tersebut”, not “pasti akan terjadi”.
5. **Do not misstate recovery.** The 2066 benchmark is Antarctic total-column ozone returning to its 1980 level under the WMO assessment assumptions; it is not a guaranteed date for a perfectly closed hole.
6. **No Indonesia-under-the-hole framing.** The Antarctic ozone hole is geographically centred over Antarctica. The ozone layer is global, but the seasonal hole is a polar phenomenon. Any local UV consequence needs separate location-specific evidence.
7. **No conflation with climate change.** Ozone depletion and global warming are different processes, although ODS can also be potent greenhouse gases and the Kigali Amendment addresses HFC climate forcing.
8. **No alarmist health forecast.** UV radiation can harm human and ecological health, but do not assign Indonesian cancer cases, crop losses, or mortality numbers without region-specific exposure and epidemiological sources.
9. **No unsupported visuals.** A dramatic world-map ozone collapse is a model visualization, not satellite footage. Label the alternate world clearly as a modelled scenario and do not imply it is a real NASA image from 2026.
10. **No rights assumption.** NASA/NOAA pages and research figures have distinct media/credit terms. Do not reuse a figure or video until its individual rights/credit and third-party material are checked.
