## 1. Creative brief
- Audience hypothesis: viewers understand thirst as an everyday sensation but may not realize it is a conscious behavioral drive coordinated with other water-balance mechanisms.
- Platform / aspect / duration: TikTok, YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels; 9:16; target 50–58 seconds.
- Core question: What if a person could no longer consciously feel thirst?
- One-sentence hypothetical premise: In a clearly fictional simulation, the conscious sensation of thirst is selectively absent while other water-balance mechanisms are not automatically assumed to fail.
- Variable changed: conscious thirst perception / behavioral urge to seek water.
- Variables held constant: no real diagnosis is assigned; AVP secretion, kidney response, baseline hydration, environmental conditions, and water loss remain separate variables rather than being silently assumed to be normal.
- Emotional objective: unsettling recognition of a hidden survival signal, followed by scientific understanding rather than panic.
- Intended takeaway: thirst is a behavioral warning system; the body has other defenses, but ongoing water losses mean renal conservation is not a permanent substitute for drinking.
- What this video is NOT claiming:
  - Not a real-time medical prediction.
  - Not a claim that all adipsia has preserved AVP or normal kidney function.
  - Not a claim that a person collapses or dies after 24 hours.
  - Not a hydration prescription or medical advice.
- Visual approach: realistic cinematic POV, water as recurring motif, restrained medical visualization, no fake clinical footage.
- AI visual disclosure: realistic AI-generated visuals should be labeled using the platform's current disclosure controls.

## 4. Scientific model
- Baseline system: thirst perception and drinking behavior operate alongside AVP-mediated renal water conservation and other regulatory mechanisms.
- Changed variable: the conscious thirst sensation is selectively absent in the fictional scenario.
- Mechanism: the brain normally integrates information about fluid concentration and circulatory state into behavioral and neuroendocrine responses. The episode does not claim that one discrete neural switch has been clinically isolated in a healthy person.
- Other defenses: AVP can promote renal water reabsorption; kidney conservation is not unlimited.
- Ongoing losses: respiration, sweating, and excretion continue.
- Consequences: the person may fail to respond behaviorally to fluid need; severity and timing cannot be universalized.
- Environmental context: heat and exertion can increase sweating and water loss, but the episode does not model a deterministic heat illness outcome.
- Known unknowns: no reviewed controlled human study validates an isolated 24-hour thirst-off condition in healthy people.
- Red-team conclusion: do not show collapse, organ failure, a dehydration percentage, a fatality, or a countdown.
