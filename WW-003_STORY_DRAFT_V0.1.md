## 2. Research update and scientific boundary

The thought experiment isolates the conscious sensation/behavioral drive of thirst while assuming that osmoregulated AVP secretion and renal response remain functional. This is a fictional scenario parameter, not a clinical diagnosis or prediction.

- Thirst is a conscious sensation and behavioral drive to seek and consume water.
- Arginine vasopressin (AVP) is an endocrine regulator of renal water excretion; AVP promotes water reabsorption through the renal collecting duct, including AQP2-related mechanisms.
- The pathways are coordinated and anatomically related; they must not be described as wholly independent.
- Loss of thirst does not, by itself, prove loss of AVP secretion or renal responsiveness.
- Adipsic AVP deficiency is a distinct clinical condition in which impaired thirst is commonly accompanied by AVP deficiency. Do not conflate it with this isolated-thirst thought experiment.
- Urine, respiration, and sweating continue to contribute to water loss. Renal conservation is not a limitless replacement for drinking.
- No universal 24-hour dehydration, collapse, organ-failure, or mortality timeline is supported. Do not use one.

## 4. Story premise

Core question: What happens when the behavioral alarm that normally prompts a person to drink goes silent, while one internal water-conservation mechanism is assumed to remain active?

Changed variable: conscious thirst perception is selectively absent for a fictional 24-hour interval.

Held constant: AVP secretion and renal responsiveness are assumed to function; ordinary physiological water losses continue.

Non-claims:
- This is not a depiction of adipsic AVP deficiency.
- It is not a medical prediction, diagnosis, or advice.
- It does not predict collapse, severe dehydration, or death at a fixed time.
- It does not imply kidneys can replace all fluid intake.

Human stake: the person can have water within reach but lacks the normal internal behavioral cue to drink.
