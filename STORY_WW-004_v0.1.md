## 1. Creative brief

- **Core question:** Bagaimana petunjuk visual yang singkat dapat membuat seseorang mengenali gambar ambigu yang sama dengan lebih mudah?
- **Story premise:** Penonton melihat gambar dua-warna yang sulit dikenali, menerima petunjuk berupa foto aslinya, lalu melihat kembali gambar dua-warna yang identik. Cerita memperlihatkan bahwa stimulus tidak berubah, tetapi pengenalan dapat berubah setelah informasi baru diperoleh.
- **Variable changed:** Informasi/prior cue yang diterima pengamat sebelum melihat ulang stimulus.
- **Variables held constant:** File gambar dua-warna, geometri/piksel gambar, ukuran tampilan, orientasi, dan kondisi presentasi dalam demonstrasi.
- **Emotional objective:** Rasa penasaran → momen “oh, sekarang terlihat” → takjub yang terukur, bukan sensasi mistis.
- **Audience hypothesis:** Penonton short-form Indonesia akan memahami fenomena lebih cepat melalui demonstrasi visual langsung daripada penjelasan abstrak tentang top-down processing.
- **Human stakes:** Pengetahuan sebelumnya dapat memengaruhi cara kita menafsirkan informasi visual yang ambigu. Batasi takeaway pada konteks persepsi visual; jangan menggeneralisasi bahwa semua persepsi atau realitas sepenuhnya subjektif.
- **Intended takeaway:** “Gambarnya tetap sama; petunjuk yang kita dapat membantu kita mengenali polanya.”
- **What this video is NOT claiming:** Mata memperoleh detail baru; otak mengubah piksel; semua orang pasti mengenali gambar setelah satu paparan; efek ini adalah halusinasi, gangguan, terapi, atau bukti bahwa realitas sepenuhnya diciptakan otak.
- **Unique angle:** Penonton mengalami urutan stimulus → petunjuk → stimulus identik, lalu mendapat penjelasan singkat dari penelitian manusia.
- **Hypothetical framing:** Ini adalah demonstrasi fenomena persepsi yang diteliti, bukan kejadian medis atau eksperimen yang sedang berlangsung di depan kamera.

## 4. Visual narrative logic (not generation prompts)

1. **Ambiguous stimulus:** one original, rights-documented grayscale photograph is transformed into a high-contrast two-tone image. Choose an object/scene that is difficult but not impossible to recognize at phone size.
2. **Cue image:** show the exact source grayscale photograph briefly and clearly enough to communicate object identity.
3. **Return stimulus:** reuse the exact same two-tone file from step 1. Do not regenerate, morph, sharpen, add edges, or alter the crop between first and second presentation.
4. **Evidence layer:** use restrained documentary treatment for the study context. No invented neural animation or literal brain-region activation unless separately validated and clearly illustrative.
5. **Closing:** hold the identical two-tone stimulus while the VO states the takeaway.

**Stimulus acceptance requirements (must be tested in pre-production):**
- Two-tone and grayscale images are derived from the same source photograph.
- The two-tone image file is byte-identical when shown before and after the cue; retain a checksum in the asset register later.
- The initial two-tone version is ambiguous to a small informal test group but not impossible for everyone; record test method and avoid presenting informal testing as scientific data.
- The grayscale cue makes the source object unambiguous.
- The two-tone image remains legible on a typical phone display in 9:16.
- Rights and generation/editing tool terms are checked before use.
- Do not use the research paper's stimuli/figures without confirming permission or exact license.

## 6. Risk and red-team review

- **Risk: visual trick is mistaken for a changed image.** Mitigation: preserve one exact two-tone file and show the unchanged return; record checksum in pre-production.
- **Risk: every viewer is expected to recognize the image.** Mitigation: phrase the prompt as a question; do not say “sekarang kamu pasti melihatnya”.
- **Risk: clinical sample is generalized to everyone.** Mitigation: VO explicitly says 13 patients undergoing epilepsy monitoring; avoid population-wide percentage.
- **Risk: neural correlation is presented as proven causal pathway.** Mitigation: use “respons berubah” and “peneliti menduga”; avoid a single brain-region explanation.
- **Risk: sensational “brain rewires instantly” claim.** Mitigation: prohibited in VO, overlays, caption, and visual metaphors.
- **Risk: stimulus rights unclear.** Mitigation: original rights-documented stimulus preferred; unresolved rights block pre-production approval.
- **Risk: the video looks like a real clinical experiment.** Mitigation: label research explanation as a demonstration of a published phenomenon; do not fabricate lab footage or patient footage.
