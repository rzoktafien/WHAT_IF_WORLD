## 1. Purpose

## 2. New source records

### SRC-014 — The neurobiology of thirst and salt appetite
- Authors: James C. R. Grove and Zachary A. Knight.
- Journal: Neuron, 112(24), 2024-12-18, pp. 3999–4016.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2024.10.028
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39610247/
- Access date: 2026-11-04.
- Type: peer-reviewed neuroscience review.
- Key support: neural systems detect changes in osmolality, blood volume, and sodium; the subfornical organ (SFO), organum vasculosum of the lamina terminalis (OVLT), and median preoptic nucleus (MnPO) participate in thirst circuitry; downstream hypothalamic regions coordinate behavioral and neuroendocrine responses including AVP; water ingestion can rapidly reduce thirst drive; renal conservation does not eliminate ongoing water losses through respiration, excretion, and sweating.
- Limitation: review includes substantial animal and circuit-level research; do not imply that a single discrete "thirst switch" in a human brain has been clinically established.

### SRC-015 — Adipsic hypernatraemia without hypothalamic structural abnormality
- Authors: Giulia Esposito, Samson Oghenetsovwe Oyibo, Satyanarayana Varma Sagi.
- Journal: BMJ Case Reports 19(10), e276016, published 2026-10-23.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1136/bcr-2026-276016
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42919603/
- Access date: 2026-11-04.
- Type: peer-reviewed clinical case report.
- Evidence inspected: PubMed record and abstract.
- Key support: a woman in her 20s had absent thirst perception and chronic severe hypernatremia; urine was concentrated and AVP response preserved; investigations excluded AVP deficiency and adipsic AVP deficiency. The report illustrates that impaired thirst can occur with preserved AVP response and concentrated urine.
- Limitation: one medically complex case with developmental and genetic history; does not represent a healthy person, does not establish prevalence, and does not validate a 24-hour scenario.

## 3. Consolidated scientific model

### FACTS supported by reviewed sources
- Thirst is a conscious sensation and behavioral drive that encourages water seeking and intake.
- The brain integrates osmotic, volume-related, hormonal, and anticipatory signals in circuits involving the SFO, OVLT, MnPO, and hypothalamic regions.
- AVP and renal water reabsorption form an important physiological defense against water loss.
- Thirst and AVP regulation are coordinated but are not identical processes; clinical cases show that their dysfunction can vary.
- Water continues to be lost through respiration, excretion, and sweating. Renal conservation is not a permanent substitute for drinking.

### FICTIONAL SCENARIO RULE
For this episode only, imagine that a selective neural disturbance prevents the person from consciously experiencing thirst. Other physiological variables are not automatically assumed to fail. The scenario does not specify a real lesion, diagnosis, or treatment.

### NOT ESTABLISHED
- No reviewed controlled human study demonstrates a healthy person's isolated thirst sensation being switched off for exactly 24 hours.
- No universal timeline to dehydration, collapse, organ injury, or death is supported.
- The episode cannot claim that every person with absent thirst has normal AVP or normal renal function.
- Heat exposure is a possible contextual modifier, not a deterministic cause of collapse in this scenario.

## 5. Red-team restrictions for the episode
- Do not use the phrase "otak berhenti mengirim semua sinyal haus" as a literal clinical diagnosis. Use it as a simplified hypothetical hook, then clarify the premise.
- Do not say the kidneys "stop working" or that vasopressin definitely remains normal in real patients.
- Do not show an exact countdown to collapse or death.
- Do not use percentages of dehydration or serum sodium values from case reports as if they describe the hypothetical viewer.
- Do not imply that thirst is the body's only hydration defense.
- Do not give individualized hydration instructions.
- Do not use the 1998 or 2026 case reports as evidence of a 24-hour progression.
- If heat is depicted, state only that sweating can add to water loss; do not claim heat alone makes the scenario fatal.
