## 1. Story promise

**Core question:** Bisakah mineral menyimpan petunjuk bahwa Venus dahulu memiliki lebih banyak air?

**One-sentence premise:** Jika mineral yang pernah terbentuk dalam lingkungan lebih kaya air dapat meninggalkan jejak kimia yang bertahan setelah terpapar atmosfer Venus, batuan Venus mungkin menyimpan petunjuk tentang evolusi air planet itu.

**Audience takeaway:** Sebuah eksperimen laboratorium menemukan bahwa fluor dapat mengubah sebagian mineral mika dalam kondisi mirip permukaan Venus. Perubahan ini berpotensi membantu mineral tertentu bertahan, tetapi petunjuk itu baru berguna untuk sejarah Venus jika mineral sejenis kelak ditemukan dan konteks geologinya dipahami.

**Emotional objective:** Rasa takjub dan penasaran—bukan ketakutan. Venus diposisikan sebagai tetangga Bumi yang evolusinya masih menyimpan teka-teki.

**What this story is NOT claiming:**
- Tidak mengklaim mineral fluoro-mika sudah ditemukan di Venus.
- Tidak mengklaim eksperimen laboratorium membuktikan Venus pernah memiliki samudra.
- Tidak mengklaim magnesite adalah bukti tunggal air purba.
- Tidak mengklaim Venus pernah dihuni atau memiliki kehidupan.
- Tidak menyajikan rekonstruksi visual sebagai rekaman asli Venus atau dokumentasi eksperimen yang benar-benar terjadi.

## 2. Scientific story model

- Baseline: permukaan Venus modern sangat panas dan bertekanan tinggi; kondisi referensi sekitar 460 °C dan 92–93 bar (CLM-043).
- Experimental evidence: enam mineral diuji selama 60 hari dalam GEER pada kondisi simulasi Venus (CLM-044).
- Main mechanism: HF dalam campuran gas bereaksi dengan phlogopite dan muscovite; sebagian gugus OH digantikan F dan terbentuk fase mika berfluor (CLM-046).
- Supporting mechanism: brucite berubah sebagian dan menghasilkan magnesite serta periclase; sisa brucite masih ada (CLM-045). This is supporting evidence only, not the narrative proof of ancient surface water.
- Conditional inference: fluorination may increase thermal stability; if comparable phases are eventually detected on Venus and their geological context is established, they may help test a past water-richer origin (CLM-047).
- Unknown: whether such phases occur naturally on Venus, where they occur, how they formed, and whether they persist over geological timescales.
- Time horizon: 60-day laboratory experiment; geological history remains unresolved.
- Spatial scope: laboratory simulation and planet-wide scientific question; no specific Venus surface location is assigned to the hypothetical mineral.
- Alternative explanations: mineral formation and alteration pathways other than ancient surface liquid water must be considered.
- Red-team lock: do not use a visual or line that implies confirmed discovery, a global ocean, habitability or life.

## 5. Visual storytelling direction (story-level only)

- Overall: realistic cinematic/scientific documentary, restrained color science, tactile mineral detail, physically plausible Venus environment.
- Color progression: mineral charcoal/amber macro → dense orange Venus atmosphere → cool neutral laboratory steel → close-up mineral structure → return to deep orange planetary scale → restrained dark planetary horizon.
- Visual hierarchy: mineral grain is the recurring visual anchor; laboratory apparatus is a clearly identified reconstruction; Venus surface is an explicitly hypothetical reconstruction.
- Scientific visualization: OH-to-F substitution may be shown as a labeled schematic molecular/structural animation, not as a literal camera view of atoms.
- No character continuity dependency in this story. No recurring human face is required; use gloved hands only if a later shot genuinely needs a scale cue. Do not assign a permanent character ID.
- No real spacecraft or agency logo is required. Future-mission references, if used in later stages, must be clearly illustrative and not depict an unverified spacecraft configuration.
- Avoid: blue oceans on ancient Venus as established fact, lush forests, lifeforms, recognizable “ancient Earth” imagery, water droplets visibly trapped inside the tested mica, or a mineral specimen presented as a confirmed Venus sample.
